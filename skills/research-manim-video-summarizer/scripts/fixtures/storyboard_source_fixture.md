# Capacity comparison source fixture

## Problem statement

Paired pile tests with identical nominal designs produced visibly different load-displacement responses. This observed mismatch motivates a comparison that retains both measured behavior and prediction.

## Methods Section 2

Each test is represented on common axes by one measured coordinate and one predicted coordinate. This representation preserves the paired values while allowing all tests to be compared geometrically.

## Methods Section 3

A diagonal benchmark relation supplies the reference required to interpret signed error. Distance from this line is a comparison measure and is not treated as a causal model.

## Methods Section 3.1

Measured capacity is defined at the visible peak of the measured response curve. Predicted capacity is selected at the corresponding peak of the comparable prediction curve, and both capacities have units of kilonewtons.

## Equation 1

The dimensionless transformation ratio is formed by dividing measured capacity by predicted capacity. Both quantities are defined before the ratio and their common unit cancels.

## Methods Section 3.2

Repeated transformation ratios are aggregated on a common number line to estimate their empirical distribution. The distribution and confidence interval retain the observed spread rather than reporting only a center.

## Results Figure 3

Two uncertainty interpretations are aligned on the same ratio axis. Their overlap and disagreement can therefore be compared without changing either location or width.

## Calibration Section 4

Calibration is applied to the endpoints of the original confidence interval. The calibrated interval is narrower while its reference center and comparison axis remain fixed.

## Conclusions Section 5

The resulting interval is returned to the original pile-capacity interpretation. The conclusion concerns capacity with uncertainty for the physical pile rather than an isolated transformed number.
